# Wellness Assessment: User Journey

## Outcome

The assessment is a supportive guided reflection that gives a visitor a useful, non-medical wellness report. It is not a diagnosis, a product recommendation, or a lead form disguised as a quiz.

## End-to-end journey

1. **Arrival:** a visitor sees a calm promise: “Understand the habits that matter most for your goal.” They can read what the assessment does, duration, privacy approach, and limits before starting.
2. **Welcome:** the engine asks their primary intention in their own terms—routine, energy, meal planning, movement, rest, or general learning. A short tailored acknowledgement confirms the choice without assuming a health problem.
3. **Conversation:** one question at a time, grouped into natural moments. Every question either selects a route, changes a score, improves recommendation relevance, or is omitted.
4. **Reflection:** after key answers, the UI may acknowledge a strength or normalize a challenge. It never displays a judgmental score mid-flow.
5. **Safety branch:** someone seeking individual medical guidance is shown a clear professional-care route and may continue only with general education; no diagnosis inference is recorded.
6. **Report:** the engine creates a respectful snapshot: strengths, patterns, 1–3 practical priorities, learning resources, recipes, and an optional next action.
7. **Optional connection:** only after seeing the report, a person may request a conversation. They choose whether their report is shared and give explicit, versioned consent.
8. **Return:** a person may save a pseudonymous report link for a defined period, revisit recommended material, or restart. No account is required in v1.

## Conversation phases

| Phase                 | Visitor experience                         | Engine purpose              | Exit criterion                |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Consent and intent    | “What would be most useful today?”         | eligibility, purpose, route | one primary goal              |
| Everyday rhythm       | sleep, work pattern, meals, movement       | identify relevant domains   | enough data for primary goal  |
| Barriers and capacity | time, confidence, support, prior attempts  | readiness and priority fit  | one realistic next-step style |
| Optional context      | body measures, preferences, coach interest | refine without blocking     | only if visitor opts in       |
| Report transition     | “Here is what you told us…”                | calculate, explain limits   | report created                |

## Trust rules

- State approximate duration truthfully; target 3–5 minutes for the core route.
- Use skip for nonessential questions and explain consequences only where useful.
- Never withhold the report to obtain contact details.
- Never mention products, business, or a coach before a relevant, voluntary choice.
- Use supportive language: “You may benefit from exploring…” not “You need to fix…”.

## Drop-off recovery

Persist a short-lived pseudonymous session only after consent. On return, offer “continue where you left off” or “start again.” Do not send reminders without a separately granted communication consent.
