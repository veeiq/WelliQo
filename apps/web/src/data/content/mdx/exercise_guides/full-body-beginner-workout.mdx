## Welcome to Your Fitness Journey

Starting a workout routine can feel overwhelming, but it doesn't have to be. The secret to long-term success isn't crushing yourself in the gym on day one; it's about building a sustainable habit that makes you feel good. This full-body workout is designed specifically for beginners. It uses only your body weight, focusing on fundamental movement patterns that translate to everyday life.

<CoachTip>
Don't worry about being perfect. Everyone starts somewhere! If a movement feels too difficult, modify it. The goal is to move, breathe, and get your heart rate up slightly.
</CoachTip>

## The Routine: 20 Minutes to Better Health

Perform each exercise for 40 seconds, followed by 20 seconds of rest. Complete the entire circuit 3 times, resting for 1 minute between circuits.

### 1. Bodyweight Squats
Squats are the king of lower body exercises. They strengthen your quads, hamstrings, and glutes while improving your core stability.
- **How to:** Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Push your hips back and down as if sitting in a chair. Keep your chest up and your knees tracking over your toes. Push through your heels to return to standing.

### 2. Incline Push-ups
Push-ups build upper body strength. Doing them on an incline (like against a wall or a sturdy chair) makes them much more accessible for beginners.
- **How to:** Place your hands slightly wider than shoulder-width on an elevated surface. Lower your chest towards the surface, keeping your body in a straight line. Push back up to the starting position.

### 3. Alternating Reverse Lunges
Lunges improve balance, coordination, and unilateral leg strength. 
- **How to:** Step backward with one foot and lower your hips until both knees are bent at a 90-degree angle. Push off your back foot to return to the start, then switch legs.

### 4. Plank 
The plank is a phenomenal exercise for building core stability and endurance, which protects your lower back.
- **How to:** Start on your forearms and toes (or knees for a modification). Keep your body in a straight line from your head to your heels. Brace your core tightly, as if you're about to be punched in the stomach.

## Listen to Your Body

Remember that some muscle soreness a day or two after working out is normal, especially when you're new to exercise. However, sharp pain is not. Always listen to your body and prioritize rest when you need it.
