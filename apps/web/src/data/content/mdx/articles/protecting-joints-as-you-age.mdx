## The Myth of Wear and Tear

For decades, we were told that our joints were like car tires: the more you use them, the faster they wear out. We now know that this "wear and tear" model of osteoarthritis is fundamentally flawed. Joints are living tissue. Unlike tires, they respond to the stress placed upon them by adapting and growing stronger.

The real culprits of joint pain as we age are usually a combination of inactivity, muscle weakness, systemic inflammation, and carrying excess body weight.

<CoachTip>
If your knees hurt when you walk, the answer isn't necessarily to stop walking. It's often to strengthen the muscles around the knee (like the quads and glutes) to provide better support and shock absorption.
</CoachTip>

## 3 Pillars of Joint Health

### 1. Motion is Lotion
Joint cartilage doesn't have a direct blood supply. It gets its nutrients through a process similar to a sponge soaking up water. When you move and put weight on a joint, you squeeze old fluid out; when you release the pressure, new, nutrient-rich synovial fluid rushes in. Low-impact activities like cycling, swimming, and brisk walking are excellent for keeping joints lubricated.

### 2. Build the Armor
Your muscles act as shock absorbers for your joints. If your muscles are weak, all the impact from walking or running goes directly into the joint structures. Resistance training is critical for joint protection. A strong muscle is a joint's best friend.

### 3. Anti-Inflammatory Nutrition
Chronic inflammation accelerates cartilage breakdown. Eating a diet rich in Omega-3 fatty acids (like salmon, chia seeds, and walnuts) can help naturally reduce inflammation. Additionally, ensuring adequate Vitamin C and considering a collagen supplement can support the structural integrity of your joints.

## Don't Accept Pain as Normal

While occasional aches are part of life, chronic joint pain is not an inevitable consequence of aging. By staying active, strengthening your muscles, and eating well, you can protect your joints for decades to come.
