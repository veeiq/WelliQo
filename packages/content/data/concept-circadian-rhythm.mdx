# The Science of the Circadian Rhythm

Your body has a master clock located in the brain, called the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN). This clock governs your "circadian rhythm"—the 24-hour cycle that tells your body when to be alert and when to sleep.

When your rhythm is aligned, you wake up energized and fall asleep easily. When it is broken, you feel exhausted all day but wired at night.

## How it works

The master clock does not have a watch. It relies on **light** and **food** to know what time it is.

When bright light enters your eyes in the morning, the clock tells your body to release a healthy pulse of cortisol (waking you up) and stops the production of melatonin (the sleep hormone).

When light fades in the evening, the clock signals the body to release melatonin again, preparing you for deep, restorative sleep.

If you look at bright screens at 11 PM, your brain thinks the sun is still up, delaying your sleep cycle and destroying your energy for the next day.
