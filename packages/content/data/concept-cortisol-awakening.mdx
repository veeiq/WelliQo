# The Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)

Cortisol often gets a bad reputation. It is famously known as the "stress hormone," associated with anxiety, belly fat, and burnout. While chronically high cortisol is dangerous, cortisol itself is not the enemy. In fact, without it, you could not survive.

Cortisol is the hormone of alertness. And the most important cortisol event of your day happens the moment you open your eyes.

## What is the CAR?

The Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR) is a sharp, rapid increase in cortisol production (often by 50% to 160%) that occurs within the first 30 to 45 minutes after you wake up.

This spike is your body's natural ignition system. The surge in cortisol raises your core body temperature, increases your blood pressure slightly, and signals every cell in your body that it is time to be active and alert. A strong CAR correlates with high daytime energy, better cognitive performance, and a robust immune system.

## A Weak CAR Causes Brain Fog

If your Cortisol Awakening Response is blunted or delayed, you will experience what is commonly known as "sleep inertia"—that heavy, groggy feeling of brain fog that can last for hours.

A weak CAR happens when your body doesn't receive the correct signals that morning has arrived. The primary trigger for the CAR is **bright light exposure to the eyes**.

If you wake up in a dark room, look at the dim light of your phone screen, and stay indoors, your brain never receives the signal to initiate the cortisol spike. You remain in a low-energy, groggy state, forcing you to rely on caffeine to simulate the alertness your body should have produced naturally.

## How to Trigger the CAR

To optimize your morning energy, you must trigger a robust CAR.
The protocol is incredibly simple: get outside. Exposing your eyes to natural sunlight within 30 minutes of waking provides the photon intensity required to signal the brain and launch the Cortisol Awakening Response, setting you up for sustained, natural energy all day long.
