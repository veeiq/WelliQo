# The 90-Minute Digital Sunset

You cannot sprint all day and expect to stop instantly when your head hits the pillow. Your brain requires a gradual down-regulation. The single biggest disruptor of this process is artificial light.

## The Protocol

**Turn off all bright screens (phones, laptops, tablets, intense televisions) 90 minutes before you intend to sleep.**

If you go to bed at 10:30 PM, your Digital Sunset begins at 9:00 PM.

## Why This Works

Your circadian rhythm relies on the fading of light to know that the day is ending. When your eyes detect darkness, a signal is sent to the pineal gland in your brain to begin producing **melatonin**, the hormone that prepares your body for deep sleep.

Modern screens emit blue and green light at intensities that mimic daylight. When you look at your phone in bed, your brain literally believes the sun is still up. This drastically suppresses melatonin production.

Even if you manage to fall asleep after looking at a screen, the _quality_ and _architecture_ of your sleep (the amount of deep and REM sleep you get) will be severely compromised. You will wake up feeling unrefreshed, triggering a cycle of low energy and caffeine dependence the next day.

## How to Execute the Sunset

Many people fail this habit because they rely on willpower and stare at the wall for 90 minutes. You must replace the screen with an analog activity.

- **Dim the Environment:** Turn off bright overhead lights. Use dim, warm lamps (red or amber light is best).
- **Read Fiction:** Reading a physical book (specifically fiction) disengages the analytical part of your brain and prepares you for dreaming.
- **Prep for Tomorrow:** Use this time to lay out your clothes, pack your lunch, or write down your tasks for the next day to clear your mind.
- **Connect:** Spend time talking with your partner or family without background television.

> [!TIP]
> **Blue Light Blockers**
> If you absolutely _must_ work late, wear high-quality, red-tinted blue-light-blocking glasses for the last 90 minutes of your evening.
